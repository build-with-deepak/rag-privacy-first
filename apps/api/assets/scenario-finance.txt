Gulf Business Services — Financial Operations Handbook

SECTION 1: ORGANIZATION OVERVIEW

Gulf Business Services provides outsourced back-office functions —
accounting, payroll administration, and vendor management — to mid-sized
clients across the UAE. This handbook covers the internal financial
policies employees and approved vendors operate under: expense
reimbursement, procurement, and the internal controls that govern who can
approve what.

SECTION 2: EXPENSE REIMBURSEMENT POLICY

An expense is reimbursable if it was incurred for a legitimate business
purpose, is supported by an itemized receipt (a credit card statement
line alone is not sufficient), and is submitted within 30 days of the
expense date. Expenses submitted after 30 days require a written
justification and manager approval before they can be processed at all —
they are not automatically rejected, but they do not follow the standard
approval path in Section 3.

Client entertainment expenses are reimbursable up to AED 500 per event
without additional approval; above that threshold, the standard approval
chain in Section 3 applies regardless of expense category. Personal
expenses, even if incurred during a business trip, are never reimbursable
— a business trip does not make an unrelated personal purchase a business
expense.

SECTION 3: APPROVAL THRESHOLDS AND CHAINS

Expenses and purchases route through approval based on amount, not
category. Under AED 1,000: approved by the employee's direct manager only.
AED 1,000 to AED 10,000: requires both the direct manager and the
department head. Above AED 10,000: requires the direct manager, the
department head, and the Finance Director. No single approver may approve
their own submitted expense or a purchase they initiated, regardless of
amount — this is a fixed control and has no exception path, because it is
the control that prevents a single person from both initiating and
approving the same spend.

SECTION 4: PROCUREMENT POLICY

A new vendor cannot be paid until procurement has completed vendor
onboarding, which requires: a signed vendor agreement, a valid trade
license on file, and banking details confirmed through a callback to the
vendor's registered phone number — not the phone number provided in the
same email as the banking details, because that callback step exists
specifically to catch business email compromise attempts where an
attacker requests a change of banking details from a compromised or
spoofed account.

Purchase orders above AED 10,000 require two competing quotes on file
before the purchase order is raised, unless the vendor is a sole-source
provider (the only vendor capable of supplying the item or service), in
which case a sole-source justification memo replaces the second quote
requirement and must be approved by the department head.

SECTION 5: VENDOR AGREEMENT BASICS

Standard vendor agreements specify payment terms of net-30 from invoice
receipt, require the vendor to carry appropriate liability insurance for
the services provided, and include a right-to-audit clause allowing Gulf
Business Services to review the vendor's records relevant to the
engagement. Payment terms shorter than net-30 require Finance Director
approval, because shortened terms reduce the company's own cash-flow
buffer and are not granted as a default courtesy.

SECTION 6: INTERNAL CONTROLS

Bank account signatories are reviewed quarterly and require two
authorized signatures for any wire transfer above AED 50,000. Below that
threshold, one signatory may release a wire transfer, but the transfer is
still logged and reviewed by the second signatory retroactively within
one business day. Any wire transfer request that changes a vendor's
previously-confirmed banking details is treated as a new vendor
onboarding event under Section 4, in full, regardless of how long the
vendor relationship has existed — an established vendor relationship is
not, by itself, sufficient reason to skip the banking-details callback.

SECTION 7: RECORD RETENTION

Financial records supporting expense reimbursements, purchase orders, and
vendor payments are retained for 7 years from the date of the
transaction, in line with UAE statutory record-keeping requirements for
this business's income tax and audit obligations. Records are stored in
the document management system with the originating transaction ID as the
retrieval key, so any transaction can be traced back to its supporting
documentation without a manual search.
