Dubai Logistics Co. — Operations Handbook

SECTION 1: COMPANY OVERVIEW

Dubai Logistics Co. operates freight and last-mile delivery across the UAE,
with regional hubs in Dubai, Abu Dhabi, Jebel Ali, and Sharjah. The company
runs a mixed fleet of company-owned vans and contracted third-party carriers,
and handles both business-to-business freight (pallets, container-scale
shipments through Jebel Ali port) and business-to-consumer last-mile
delivery. This handbook is the reference the operations team uses to decide
what to do when a shipment does not go as planned.

SECTION 2: SHIPPING SOP

Every shipment is assigned a tracking ID in the format DXB-#### at the point
of booking. A shipment moves through five states in order: booked, at
origin warehouse, in transit, at destination warehouse, delivered. A
shipment cannot skip a state — if a shipment shows "in transit" without ever
having shown "at origin warehouse," that is a data error to be corrected
before any customer-facing status is given. Drivers update shipment state
from a handheld scanner at each warehouse gate; manual state changes by
dispatch staff must include a note explaining why the automated scan was
not used.

International shipments (crossing into Oman, Saudi Arabia, or further) add
a customs-clearance sub-state between "in transit" and "at destination
warehouse." Customs clearance is the single largest source of delay on
international lanes and is not within the company's direct control, but
dispatch is still responsible for proactively notifying the customer once
a shipment has been in customs-clearance for more than 24 hours.

SECTION 3: WAREHOUSE SOP

Each regional warehouse operates with a target capacity utilization of no
more than 85%. When a warehouse's dashboard shows utilization above 85%,
incoming shipments should be rerouted to the nearest warehouse with
available capacity rather than queued, because queuing at a full warehouse
is the most common root cause of downstream delivery delays. Warehouse
staff are required to log any pallet or package showing visible damage on
arrival, before it is shelved — damage logged after shelving cannot be
reliably attributed to transit versus warehouse handling, which matters for
insurance claims (see Section 6).

SECTION 4: DELIVERY SLA

The standard delivery SLA is 48 hours from "at destination warehouse" to
"delivered" for last-mile shipments within the same emirate, and 72 hours
for cross-emirate last-mile delivery. International freight SLAs are
quoted per shipment at booking time and are not covered by this standard
window, because customs clearance timing varies too much to commit to a
fixed number.

A shipment is considered at risk of breaching its SLA once it has used 75%
of its allotted window without reaching "delivered." At-risk shipments are
automatically flagged on the operations dashboard and should be checked
for an available alternative vehicle or driver before the SLA is actually
breached, not after.

SECTION 5: CUSTOMER ESCALATION POLICY

When a shipment misses its delivery window, the operations team follows
this sequence: first, check the shipment's current state and the reason
for delay (customs, warehouse capacity, vehicle breakdown, or driver
unavailability); second, check whether an alternative vehicle or driver is
available at the shipment's current location; third, if an alternative is
available, reroute and notify the customer with a revised ETA; fourth, if
no alternative is available, open a customer escalation case and offer a
choice between waiting for the original vehicle or a partial delivery of
what is available.

A customer escalation case must be created (not just a note added to the
shipment) whenever a delay exceeds 4 hours past the SLA window, because
escalation cases are what trigger the compensation policy in Section 6 and
are tracked separately by the customer support team.

SECTION 6: INSURANCE AND DAMAGE CLAIMS

All shipments are insured against loss and damage in transit through the
company's insurance vendor, Gulf Cargo Assurance. A damage claim requires:
the shipment's tracking ID, a warehouse-logged damage note (see Section 3)
or a delivery-time damage report signed by the recipient, and photos of the
damaged item. Claims without a warehouse-logged damage note and without a
signed delivery-time report cannot be processed, because there is no way
to establish when in the shipment's journey the damage occurred. Approved
claims are settled within 10 business days of the vendor's confirmation.

SECTION 7: VENDOR AGREEMENTS

Third-party carriers used for overflow capacity during peak season operate
under a standard vendor agreement requiring: proof of insurance coverage
matching or exceeding the company's own policy, GPS tracking integration
with the company's dispatch system, and a maximum sub-contracted delivery
window equal to the company's own SLA in Section 4 — a third-party carrier
cannot offer a customer a longer window than the company's own standard
without written approval from the operations manager.
